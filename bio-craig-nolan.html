Craig Nolan is a trial attorney, appellate advocate, and former federal prosecutor who represents clients at trial and in government investigations and enforcement matters, white collar defense, commercial disputes, product liability litigation, regulatory matters, and appeals.

With more than 30 jury trials and dozens of appeals before federal appellate and state supreme courts, Craig has served as White Collar Crimes Chief at the U.S. Attorney's Office for Hawaii, State's Attorney for Vermont's third-largest county, and a civil litigator at leading firms in Vermont and North Carolina.

Craig brings the perspective of a federal prosecutor, trial attorney, and appellate advocate to matters that require early control, nuanced judgment, and demonstrable trial-readiness. He is especially valuable where litigation risk, government scrutiny, and appellate issues overlap, and where trial counsel is needed to assist or replace litigation counsel.

REPRESENTATIVE EXPERIENCE

Craig Nolan's experience includes:

  • Served as White Collar Crimes Chief and Branch Chief as an Assistant U.S. Attorney in Hawaii and Vermont, respectively, during 15-year DOJ career.
  • Represented Valero Energy Corp. in groundwater MTBE-contamination litigation by the State of Vermont.
  • Represented a construction management company in the $200 million Jay Peak EB-5 fraud investigation by the Department of Justice.
  • Represented a defendant in SEC enforcement litigation involving insider-trading allegations connected to Keurig Green Mountain stock.
  • Represented Vermont Gas before the Vermont Supreme Court in multiple appeals from the Public Utilities Commission.
  • Tried 26 matters to jury verdict and dozens of bench trials and administrative evidentiary hearings.
  • Served as appellate counsel in dozens of appeals in federal and state supreme courts, including the Second Circuit, Ninth Circuit, and Vermont Supreme Court.
  • Brought former-prosecutor judgment to investigations, enforcement defense, and complex civil matters where early strategy and controlled communication matter.
